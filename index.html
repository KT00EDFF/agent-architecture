The three roles

Every multi-agent system is a division of labor. These are the three archetypal jobs. Real systems mix and nest them.

Role 1

Supervisor Agent

The decision-maker at the top. It receives the user's goal, decides who should handle it, delegates, and synthesizes the results into a final answer. It owns the conversation and the outcome — but does little domain work itself.

Thinks about

  • "Which agent is right for this?"
  • "Do I have enough to answer?"
  • "Should I ask a follow-up?"

Superpower / risk

  • ✅ Flexible routing, easy to extend
  • ⚠️ Becomes a bottleneck & single point of failure
  • ⚠️ Can loop if it can't decide

Analogy: an ER triage doctor — doesn't do the surgery, decides which specialist you see.

Role 2

Orchestrator Agent

The workflow engine. Where a supervisor decides who, an orchestrator runs a plan: it breaks a goal into ordered steps, calls agents in sequence (or in parallel), passes outputs of one step as inputs to the next, and handles retries and failures. It cares about process, not just routing.

Thinks about

  • "What are the steps, in what order?"
  • "What can run in parallel?"
  • "Step 2 failed — retry or reroute?"

Superpower / risk

  • ✅ Reliable, repeatable pipelines
  • ✅ State & error handling
  • ⚠️ Rigid; less adaptive than pure routing

Analogy: a film director working from a shot list — sequences the specialists, keeps continuity between scenes.

Role 3

Sub-agent / Domain Agent

The specialist that does the actual work. Narrow scope, deep capability: a SQL agent, a code-review agent, a billing agent, a web-research agent. It's given a focused sub-task with just the context it needs, and returns a focused result. Good sub-agents are replaceable and testable in isolation.

Thinks about

  • "Do this one thing well"
  • "Use my tools for this domain"
  • "Return a clean, structured result"

Superpower / risk

  • ✅ Focused context = fewer mistakes
  • ✅ Independently upgradable
  • ⚠️ No global view; can't self-coordinate

Analogy: a cardiologist — brilliant in one domain, relies on triage to be sent the right patients.

How they fit together

These roles nest. A common production shape is: Supervisor → Orchestrator → Domain sub-agents. The supervisor picks the workflow, the orchestrator runs its steps, and each step is a specialized sub-agent. Sometimes the supervisor is the orchestrator; sometimes a sub-agent is itself a supervisor over its own team (recursion).

Supervisor vs Orchestrator — the key distinction

  • Supervisor = dynamic routing. Decides at runtime.
  • Orchestrator = defined process. Follows a plan (which may be generated).
  • Routing answers "who?"; orchestration answers "in what order, with what handoffs?"

Design rules of thumb

  • Keep sub-agent context narrow — pass the task, not the whole history.
  • Prefer many small specialists over one do-everything agent.
  • Make handoffs structured (typed outputs) so steps compose.
  • Give the top agent a clear stop condition.
